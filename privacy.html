Alma — Privacy

Last updated: 23 September 2026

This page says what Alma keeps about you, where, and who can see it. When any of it stops being true, this page changes before the app does.

What changed on 23 September 2026: you can now choose to keep a copy of your record, so it comes back if you lose or change your phone. You are asked first, and it is off unless you say yes. Reports about messages are now read by a person. And you can delete your account from a web page, without the app.

The short version

  • Danger signs and finding a health facility work without an account and without a signal. The rest of Alma needs you to sign in once, and then works with no signal.
  • Your record lives on your phone. A copy is kept on Alma's server only if you choose it — and then only your account can open it.
  • On a shared phone, your record opens only for your account.
  • Nothing about HIV is ever stored on Alma's server — not a result, and not even whether your status is known.
  • Your location stays on the phone, except when Alma works out a route to a facility you opened — and then only the two points, to a mapping service that is not told who you are.
  • You can delete everything, and deleted means deleted — in the app, or from the web.

What you can do without an account

These are on the sign-in screen itself, and work with no account and no signal:

  • Danger signs — the "something is wrong" answers
  • Finding a health facility near you

Once you are signed in, all of this works on the phone alone, with no signal:

  • Your health record, your dates and your visit schedule
  • Your medicines and why you are taking them
  • Your check-ins, your scans and your reminders

Nothing you need in a hurry waits for a network.

What an account is for

1. Your record opens only for you. On a shared phone, somebody else who signs in with their own account is not shown it. 2. The community — talking to other mothers at your stage. 3. A copy of your record, if you want one, so it survives losing your phone.

What Alma stores about you

On your phone

Your record — your name, dates and stage, your visits and measurements, your check-ins and what you wrote with them, your scans, the things you have ticked off (an iron tablet taken today, a baby's immunisation visit), your saved answers, your questions for the next visit, and the conversations you had with Ask Alma.

On Alma's server, always

  • Your email address and your account, so that you can sign in.
  • What you post in the community (see below), under your first name.
  • The friends you make there, and the private messages between you.

On Alma's server, only if you choose to keep a copy

The first time you open Alma after signing in, it asks: "Keep a copy of your record?" If you say yes, a copy of your record is kept on Alma's server, so that signing in on a new phone brings it back. If you say no, nothing is kept. You can change your mind at any time in Me → A copy of your record, and remove the copy from there.

The copy contains your record as it is on your phone — including your scan pictures, each sent once — except:

  • your Ask Alma key, if you added one;
  • anything about HIV — not even whether your status is known;
  • other mothers' messages;
  • messages this phone has not managed to send yet.

Who can read it: you. Access is enforced by the database itself, row by row, against your account — not by the app, and not by anybody at Alma choosing not to look. Room admins and other mothers cannot see it.

If you have two phones, the one that saved most recently is the record. A new phone that has never been backed up never replaces a copy kept from your old phone without asking you which one to keep.

What Alma does not store, ever

  • Your HIV result. Alma records only whether your status is known, on your phone, never what it was — and even that never leaves the phone. There is nowhere in the app or the database to put a result. The Ministry of Health antenatal card marks that section privacy sensitive, and a phone is far easier to read over a shoulder than a folded card is.
  • Whether you take HIV medicine. The medicines page shows HIV medicine and Septrin to every woman, folded under "Medicines only some women take". Alma never asks whether they are yours, and keeps nothing that would say.
  • Your surname, your village or your facility in the community. There is no column to put them in.
  • Your payment details. If you subscribe, the payment is handled by Google Play. Alma is told whether you have an active subscription. It never sees a card or a mobile money PIN.

The community

In the community you are a first name and a stage. Nothing else is shown with a post.

  • Rooms are public to every signed-in mother. What you write there can be read by anybody in the room.
  • Private messages are between you and one friend, and nobody else can read them — see "Reports" below for the one exception, which only you can make.
  • Photos you send are kept on Alma's server for 30 days so the other person can receive them, and then deleted from the server automatically. They stay on the phones that received them. The rules of every room ask for no faces and no names in photos.
  • Voice messages can be sent between friends only, never in a room. Alma asks for the microphone the first time you tap it in a friend chat, and uses it only while you are recording. A voice message is kept on Alma's server for 30 days, like a photo, and then deleted from it.
  • You can delete your own messages at any time.
  • A post is saved on your phone first and sent when there is a signal, so it never disappears because the network did.

Reports

If a message could hurt someone, you can report it. What happens:

  • It is hidden for you at once, and stays hidden.
  • A person at Alma reads it. Advice that could hurt someone, and telling someone to stop a medicine, are read first.
  • The person who wrote it is never told who reported it, and the reviewer is never shown who reported it either.
  • Reporting a private message shows the reviewer that one message and nothing else — not what you wrote, and nothing before or after it. Only the person a message was sent to can report it.

A reviewer can keep a message, hide or remove it, or stop its author posting. Someone who is stopped from posting can still use Alma for her own care: her record, the danger signs and finding a facility are never taken away. Every decision is logged.

Where you are

Alma asks for your location for one reason: to sort the facility list by how far away each place is, and to put you on the map. That happens entirely on your phone. The list of facilities is downloaded once and then lives on the device.

Your location is not stored, not on the phone and not on Alma's server, and it is never attached to your record.

When your location leaves the phone

When you open a facility, Alma works out the route to it along the roads. That needs the whole road network and cannot be done on a phone, so it is asked of a mapping service.

At that moment, two pairs of coordinates are sent: where you are and where you are going. Nothing else goes with them — not your name, not your account, not that you are pregnant. There is a test in Alma's code that fails if anything else is ever added to that request.

The service is Valhalla, run by FOSSGIS e.V., a German non-profit that hosts OpenStreetMap infrastructure.

While the Near me map or a facility screen is open, Alma also follows your position and reads the phone's compass, so the arrow showing you moves as you walk. That happens on the phone and is not sent anywhere. Both stop the moment you leave the screen.

To stop all of this, turn off location permission for Alma in your phone's settings. The facility list still works; you lose the distances and routes.

Films

Alma's short films play from YouTube, through YouTube's own player shown inside Alma, because the organisations that made them allow that and do not allow copies. When you press play, YouTube (Google) receives the request from your phone and can see that this phone watched that film. Alma sends YouTube nothing about you. The small pictures on the film cards are fetched from YouTube once and then kept on your phone.

Asking Alma anything

Alma answers most questions from what is on your phone, with no internet and no cost. There is a setting, off until you turn it on, that lets Alma also answer questions nobody wrote down. It does that by sending the question to a model run by Google, through Alma's server.

What goes with the question: your week of pregnancy or how long ago you gave birth, your baby's age, anything you have told Alma needs extra care, and the pages from Alma that answer the question. Not your name, not your phone number, not where you are, and never anything about HIV.

The danger signs do not depend on any of this. They are matched on the phone, from your own words, whether the setting is on or off and whether or not you have signal.

Notifications

Reminders are created and scheduled by your own phone. Nothing about what you are reminded of is sent anywhere.

Friends. So that you hear when a mother asks to be your friend, or a friend writes to you, while Alma is closed, your phone asks Alma's server about every fifteen minutes whether anything is new. It asks with a key that belongs to this phone and can do nothing else — not your sign-in — and the answer is only how many, and a first name. A notification never shows what anybody wrote. You can turn this off in Me → Notifications → Your friends; signing out removes the key.

If you tell Alma the phone is shared, notifications say only "Alma", with no detail, so a reminder cannot tell somebody else that you are pregnant before you have.

Deleting everything

In the app: Me → Delete everything. It removes your record from this phone — including your scan pictures — and deletes your account from Alma's server, with everything that belongs to it: the copy of your record, your messages in the rooms, your private messages, your friendships and your reports. Where another mother quoted you in a reply, the quote is removed and her reply stays. A room you made passes to another of its admins or members rather than being deleted for everyone in it.

Without the app: open the deletion page at keromadurain1.github.io/alma-site/delete-account.html, enter your email, and type the code Alma sends you. It does the same thing.

Either way, this is not a request you send and wait on. It happens when you confirm it. Photos you sent in the last 30 days are deleted from the server automatically within that time.

Children

Alma is for mothers and expectant mothers. It does not knowingly collect anything from children.
